The reality of homesickness

Missing home is a common feeling among international students. This applies not only in the Australian context but also around the world. Studies on the issue indicate that:

60–65%

face loneliness or isolation among students in Australia

(Maharaj et al., 2025)
97%

stayed connected yet are still homesick globally

(HSBC, 2019)

This motivates us to initiate the project, as homesickness remains an issue international students tackle in their daily settings despite staying connected

Our story

Organised under MUISS, we understand that homesickness is one of the problems that international students usually face, especially at Christmas, when some decide not to go back home because of extended exams or extra paid work. Therefore, we organised our event to acknowledge students' feelings of missing family, friends, food, culture and everyday life back home. Therefore, by using...

It’s okay…”

... as part of our campaign name, we want to convey comfort and empathy, as well as normalise homesickness rather than framing it as an adaptation failure. This will ensure that students' concerns are heard and addressed accordingly.

Our direction

Mission

Build a supportive community for international students to gather, have dinner and share each other's stories by organising The Christmas Table to bring students together over dinner. Beyond the offline Christmas Table, we also promote an online Q&A activity for international students to open up about their feelings.

Vision

Contribute to the Australian higher education market by strengthening its positioning as a multicultural hub that encourages international students to join us and choose Australia as the start of their youth journey. We have organised the gathering event for international students as our first step towards this long-term vision of contributing to education.

Value

Supportive, inclusive and respectful are the core values behind our campaign. By supporting international students' feelings, we understand that this helps them feel safe enough to engage, join in and share their authentic feelings. Regardless of where they come from, these values are our constant reminder to help our peers.

The Christmas Table

A free multicultural cooking workshop for students who can't get home this Christmas

About the table

The Christmas Table provides a free multicultural cooking workshop for international students staying in Australia over Christmas, especially those who can't get home this year.

Everyone brings one ingredient that reminds them of home and drops it into a mystery box. In small mixed groups, you draw ingredients from the box, find out where they come from, and turn them into a dish no one has cooked before. Then we all sit down together at one long table for a Christmas meal

Every recipe and story from the day goes into a community recipe zine, a keepsake you can take home and share.

Why does it matter

At the end of the year, home means a table full of food and warm laughter. But here Christmas lands in the middle of the summer break. The campus goes quiet and family photos miss your face. If your heart feels a little heavy, please remember you are not alone. Sometimes, missing home is simply craving a familiar taste, a touch of your mum's seasoning or a childhood holiday treat. Bring that taste with you, share your story, and sit down with us.

Missing home isn't a sign of weakness, it just shows how deeply you care about a place you love. This Christmas, you don't have to carry that feeling by yourself.

About the box

Mystery box

Four cards are waiting inside

Tap the box to lift the lid. Each card explains one part of the day: how to start, what to bring, what we provide and the rules of the table.

Card 1 · The box

One box. Forty ingredients. Forty stories.

Everyone brings one ingredient that tastes like home, and nobody knows what they'll get. A jar of sambal might end up next to Sichuan peppercorns and a packet of dried mushrooms. We will allocate you into a team where your job is to turn them into one dish and learn where each ingredient comes from along the way.

Card 2 · What to bring

What to bring

  • One ingredient that reminds you of home: a spice, sauce, paste, tea, dried noodles, a snack
  • Shelf-stable please, so nothing needs a fridge
  • Sealed and labelled with your ingredient tag

Card 3 · What we provide

What we provide

  • A shared pantry: rice, noodles, eggs, tofu, seasonal vegetables, oil, salt and basic sauces
  • Cooking stations, utensils and an experienced volunteer at each station

Card 4 · The rules

The rules

  • Each group draws three ingredients from the box
  • Before cooking, guess where each one comes from. Then the owner tells you the real story.
  • Use all three ingredients, plus anything from the shared pantry
  • You have 60 minutes to cook one dish
  • Give your dish a name that mixes its origins, and write the recipe card

In the zine

Dear friends,

Have you ever missed home because of a familiar smell, a favourite dish, or a meal you used to share with someone you love?

This little book brings together five recipes and five stories from international students who joined The Christmas Table cooking workshop.

Each team received a Mystery Box filled with unexpected ingredients and created a dish inspired by their culture, their memories, and their imagination.

These are not traditional recipes. They are new creations made with teamwork, laughter, and a little bit of home.

Tango on the Beach

Hi guys!!!! We are Rolling Pandas, we YOLOed our way through the table, had an absolute blast, and made every moment count! No regrets, just good vibes and great memories!!!

When we opened our Mystery Box, we couldn't stop laughing.

“Mango, cheese, and rice paper? What are we supposed to make with these?”
We decided to try making summer rolls, even though none of us had ever combined these ingredients before.

Our first few rolls completely fell apart, and we made such a mess trying to wrap everything together. But after a lot of laughter and teamwork, we finally created something colourful and surprisingly delicious.

They weren't anything like the traditional rolls we knew, but that made them even more special. We came here to make food, but we ended up making memories together.

Berry Onigiri

Konnichiwa, everyone!!! We honestly had no idea what to do when we saw strawberries, avocado, and rice in our mystery box.

“Strawberries in onigiri? Are we really doing this?” Well, apparently, we were!

We started experimenting with different fillings, but shaping the rice turned out to be harder than we expected. Some looked like snowballs, while others completely fell apart.

Yuki showed the team how their mum used to shape onigiri back home, and suddenly, we were all learning something new. Our onigiri might not have looked perfect, but we were pretty proud of them.

Who knew a few strange ingredients could bring us so much laughter and a little feeling of home?

Cheesy Crunch

We were so relieved when we found kimchi in our mystery box. At least we recognised something! But then we discovered potatoes, corn, flour, and mozzarella.
After a little brainstorming, we decided to make a cheesy kimchi potato pancake.

Everything was going great until we tried to flip it. The cheese started spilling everywhere, and our beautiful pancake nearly turned into a disaster!

We couldn't stop laughing, but somehow, we managed to save it. When we finally took our first bite, we were genuinely surprised by how good it tasted. It wasn't the perfect dish, but cooking, laughing, and sharing it made the evening feel a little more like home.

We’re the Chessy Crew! We came, we cooked, we cheesed it up!

Coco-Pumpkin

Co to the co to the pump to the kin, that's our dish.

We thought making pasta would be easy. Then we opened our mystery box. Pumpkin, mushrooms, coconut milk, and lemon?

“Coconut milk in pasta? Seriously?”

We weren't convinced, but we decided to give it a try anyway. At first, we had no idea whether the flavours would work together. We kept tasting the sauce, adding a little of this and a little of that. To our surprise, it turned out creamy, slightly sweet, and actually really delicious!

We joked that an Italian grandmother might be shocked by our creation. It definitely wasn't a traditional Italian recipe, but it became our own little Christmas memory.

FAQ

I can't cook. Can I still come?

No worries! Cooking skill is not required, as we have a volunteer at each table to help you, so you can enjoy your meal and a warm conversation with us.

What ingredients should I bring?

Feel free to bring anything shelf-stable that reminds you of home: a spice, sauce, paste, tea or snack. Seal it and attach your ingredient tag so that we can navigate different guests' preferences as well.

How can I let you know if I have allergies or dietary needs?

Let us know in the registration. There is a section for you to note it. Every ingredient is labelled, and groups are matched to suit everyone's needs.

Can I bring a friend to the gathering?

Yes, absolutely! If they're also spending Christmas in Australia, remember to ask your friend to register as well, so it is easier for us to organise the event venue.

Will my photo or story be shared?

Only if you say yes. We highly value our guests' personal privacy, so you'll get a sticker on arrival if you'd rather not be photographed.

I can't make it. Can I still take part?

Yes, please. Send us the recipe from home and its story, and we will turn it into a zine.

What if the day gets emotional?

We hear you. Missing home is what this day is about. There's a quiet space at the venue, and someone from a wellbeing service will be there to listen, talk and give you a warm hug.

Media release

New Melbourne campaign uses food and storytelling to break mental health stigma among international students

Melbourne, VIC, Australia

About the campaign - It's okay to miss home

The campaign is community-focused and designed to support international students in Melbourne aged 18-25 in getting over homesickness and improving their mental health. By blending digital engagement on Instagram and TikTok, campus activations, and interactive community events, the campaign seeks to destigmatise mental health, build peer connections, and advocate for tailored wellbeing policies across Australian tertiary institutions.

Launched ahead of the summer holidays, the campaign calls on students, educational institutions, and authorities to view homesickness as a shared community issue requiring support, rather than a sign of personal weakness.

Psychological distress affects 31.6% - 54% of international students in Australia, while loneliness and isolation can reach 65%. Yet many international students remain reluctant to access formal counselling due to cultural stigma, family pressure and fear of speaking out.

To address this gap, “The Christmas Table” will bring international students together in Melbourne during the holiday period for a multicultural cooking workshop. Participants will bring a mystery ingredient from their home country, work in small multicultural groups to create new dishes, and share stories about their culture and experiences away from home.

Following the event, participants’ recipes and stories will be compiled into a community Recipe Zine and shared through Instagram and TikTok to encourage wider conversations about peer support, belonging and mental wellbeing. The campaign calls on local councils, Study Melbourne and university wellbeing offices to explore more culturally responsive and community - led approaches to international student wellbeing.
